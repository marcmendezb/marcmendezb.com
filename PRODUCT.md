# PRODUCT.md

**register:** brand

## Product purpose

A single-page personal landing whose only job is to get Marc Mendez hired in
Zürich. It is not a portfolio archive and not a blog. Success is one email.

## Users

Primarily **Swiss recruiters and hiring managers** — often non-technical,
scanning dozens of candidates, deciding in under thirty seconds. They screen for
three things before anything else: can this person legally work here, when can
they start, and will a Barcelona-based applicant genuinely relocate.

Secondarily **technical and design leads** who will open the repo. For this
audience the site itself is the work sample: the craft of the page is the
argument, more than anything written on it.

**Never describe Marc as an engineer.** Not "growth engineer", not
"engineering". He doesn't use the word for himself. Say builds, building,
development, ships.

## Brand voice

Three words: **precise, plainspoken, unhurried.**

Swiss International Style — the actual lineage, not a pastiche of it. Grid,
hairlines, enormous grotesque headline, generous white space, no ornament. The
confidence comes from what is left out.

Copy is declarative and short. No superlatives, no "passionate about", no
adjectives doing a verb's work.

## Anti-references

- The AI-generated SaaS landing page: gradient mesh, glass cards, icon-heading-
  text grids, a hero metric row.
- The editorial-magazine lane: display serif italic, drop caps, tiny tracked
  uppercase kickers above every section. Saturated, and wrong for this voice.
- Developer-portfolio clichés: terminal windows, matrix rain, a typing-effect
  headline, "console.log('hello')".
- Motion for its own sake. Anything that makes a recruiter wait.

## Strategic principles

0. **Marketing leads.** Seven years of marketing against four of building; the
   marketing half is both the longer track record and the thing that makes the
   building unusual. A developer who ships is common. Someone who ships the
   product *and* the funnel is the whole pitch.

   **Frame that as coherence, never as headcount.** "Three roles for one hire"
   and "most teams hire twice for that" read as an offer to be bought cheaply,
   and they price the work by what it replaces instead of what it's worth. The
   real argument is that the decisions hold together because one person makes
   them: the product and the funnel get designed together rather than
   negotiated between teams.
1. **The three screening facts stay above the fold.** Location, availability,
   work eligibility. Never buried in prose.
2. **Craft is the argument.** Performance, accessibility and motion quality are
   load-bearing evidence, not polish. A janky page contradicts the copy.
3. **Never claim what isn't verified.** Legally sensitive or unverifiable fields
   render as nothing rather than as a guess.
4. **Restraint over decoration** — but restraint with intent. Safe is invisible.
